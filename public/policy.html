When an employee badges into a SOC, the approval was entering the SOC — they then operate inside an already-approved environment. That's why "Context" is arguably the wrong mental model. It's closer to a Security Zone, an Operational Mode, or an Execution Environment.

02 · What a policy looks like

Policy becomes a pass, not a prompt.

Instead of a rule per tool call, the policy describes an environment: who may enter, what lives inside, what data class it operates on, where output may land, and how long it stays open.

Once admitted… you're done. The remaining work happens inside the envelope that was already approved.

03 · The nuance

Some actions still get their own gate.

Admission to a Context should not, by itself, authorize an irreversible act. The escape hatch is narrow and deliberate: high-consequence actions invoke additional policy.

06 · The claim worth defending
The default authorization unit is the Context, not the individual tool invocation.

Context admission establishes the operating environment, and only exceptional or destructive actions require additional approval.

One caution

Don't claim "no action approvals ever." The stronger position preserves the seamless workflow while still accommodating organizations that legitimately want an extra checkpoint for irreversible or highly privileged operations.
